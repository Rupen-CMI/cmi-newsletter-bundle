Global Drone Market

JSW has entered the Indian commercial electric vehicle market with Ampstar. This signals growing interest in electric light commercial vehicles (LCVs) and buses. New entrants typically target urban logistics and fleet operators first. For BF, this may increase demand for components from suppliers who can meet electric vehicle specifications. So what: BF should track Ampstar's launch and similar entrants as they scale production.

What is changing
JSW Launches Ampstar Electric Commercial Vehicles
Analyst Opinion
  • Ampstar's entry is a live test of whether LCV buyers will pay for electric range and fast charging. BF should map its own electric component offer against what Ampstar and other new entrants specify.
  • New EV fleets tend to buy components in smaller volumes than automotive OEMs do. BF should expect order books to fill slowly and negotiate price terms that fit low-volume runs.
  • BF's defence and aerospace forging work gives it a baseline on precision materials. That work can help it win component lines where electric vehicle specs push on tolerances.

Adroit Industries

Adroit Industries filed for an IPO, according to a September 22 report from HDFC Sky. The listing would bring a public market valuation to a company that makes aerospace components, including drone parts. This signals growing interest in Indian aerospace suppliers as demand for drones rises. It also shows investors betting on the sector's growth potential.

Who is moving
It matters because it validates the aerospace component supply chain in India, which supports the broader drone ecosystem.
What it signals
Increased capital and visibility for Indian drone component makers.
Analyst Opinion
  • Bharat Forge should expect more aerospace component tenders as Indian defence modernisation widens beyond air force drones. Adroit's listing will raise the profile of local suppliers building similar parts. BF's own defence platform work can attract new contract discussions.
  • BF should prepare its aerospace quality and delivery story for defence and industrial buyers before tender windows open. A listed rival will have clearer visibility into order volumes and timelines. BF's forging depth gives it a strong base, but defence qualification takes time.
  • BF's automotive customer base remains the anchor, so near-term revenue growth should come there first. Diversification into defence and energy still needs capacity and tooling investment. Automotive demand is steady enough to fund that build-out without slowing.

● Competition Outlook · Commercial vehicle and passenger car manufacturers

Thyssenkrupp

Thyssenkrupp has entered India's green hydrogen manufacturing market through a partnership with thyssenkrupp nucera and BHEL. The alliance aims to build facilities that produce green hydrogen, a clean fuel used in industrial processes and mobility. Thyssenkrupp is expanding beyond its traditional steel and engineering businesses into new energy infrastructure. For BF, this signals a rival gaining ground in green hydrogen, which could compete with BF's forged components in automotive and industrial applications.

Why it matters
Thyssenkrupp is using its global brand and local partners to enter a fast-growing clean energy sector, potentially competing with BF for component supply in hydrogen-powered vehicles and industrial equipment.
Their edge
Global scale, engineering expertise, and partnerships with established Indian manufacturers.
Analyst Opinion
  • Thyssenkrupp is now a direct competitor in green hydrogen, so BF should expect tighter pricing on related forging work. BF's automotive and industrial customers may ask for cost comparisons with green-hydrogen alternatives, even if those alternatives are still experimental.
  • BF should track where Thyssenkrupp builds its green-hydrogen capacity, because that will show which regions face supply pressure on forgings. If Thyssenkrupp clusters its plants near its own hydrogen sites, BF's local mills may gain leverage on delivery terms.
  • BF should position its defence and aerospace forging as a higher-value alternative to green-hydrogen systems for military mobility. Artillery and defence platforms need forged components that green hydrogen alone cannot replace, so BF can pitch those capabilities directly to defence buyers.
