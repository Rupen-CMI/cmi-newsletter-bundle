Potential Customer
● Potential Customer · Commercial vehicle and passenger car manufacturers

Kia India

Kia India launched a digital vehicle financing platform on September 22, according to ET Auto. The company also rolled out Kia Quick Finance across its dealership network. This follows its announcement of a new 536-acre manufacturing facility in Andhra Pradesh, which began trial production in January 2019 and mass production of the Kia Seltos in July 2019. With more vehicles rolling off the line, Kia will need reliable forged and machined components. That creates a clear opening for BF to supply parts for its growing India operations.

Why now
The financing platform and new manufacturing capacity mean Kia is expanding credit access and production, so component demand is rising now.
Who to target
Head of Corporate Development, VP of Strategy.
Analyst Opinion
  • Kia's new financing platform and new plant mean more vehicles, so BF should expect stronger demand for forged and machined parts sooner than later. The financing makes it easier for buyers to take delivery, so BF should position its supply chain now.
  • BF should target Kia's India operations as a priority account, starting with the Seltos platform. The existing 536-acre site in Andhra Pradesh is already producing, so BF can move samples and quotes quickly without waiting for a greenfield launch.
  • BF should also treat the defence and aerospace opportunities as a secondary but real opening. Defence platforms often use forged components, and a supplier with automotive experience tends to fit that work well.

Industry Outlook

JSW Group Enters Electric Commercial Vehicle Market

JSW Group has entered the electric commercial vehicle market through a new brand called Ampstar. The company is investing Rs 2,500 crore in this venture. This signals growing interest in electric vans and light commercial vehicles in India. New entrants often need reliable component suppliers early in their production cycle. Bharat Forge can position itself as a partner for Ampstar's component needs.

Analyst Opinion
  • Ampstar's component needs will be real, not aspirational. A Rs 2,500 crore investment is not a marketing campaign. It requires forgings, axles, and structural parts from day one.
  • Bharat Forge should engage Ampstar early, not wait for a tender. New entrants value reliability more than price in the first year. A pre-production dialogue can set expectations and volumes.
  • Defence and aerospace contracts will shape your offer to Ampstar. Bharat Forge already serves defence platforms, so it can point to that experience in its proposal.

Competition Outlook
● Competition Outlook · Renewable Energy & Electric Mobility

Thyssenkrupp

Thyssenkrupp Nucera and BHEL have formed a green hydrogen manufacturing alliance in India. The partnership will leverage BHEL's engineering expertise and Thyssenkrupp's technology. This move signals Thyssenkrupp's push into India's green hydrogen economy, a space where BF does not currently compete. For BF, this is a potential entry point for green hydrogen components, but it also highlights Thyssenkrupp's broader industrial ambitions.

Why it matters
Thyssenkrupp is expanding into India's green hydrogen market, which could create new demand for components that BF currently supplies to defence and aerospace customers.
Their edge
Global technology and local partnerships
Analyst Opinion
  • BF should treat Thyssenkrupp as a technology partner, not a competitor, in green hydrogen. Thyssenkrupp's alliance with BHEL shows it is building local engineering depth, which BF needs to match if it enters the same space.
  • BF's existing automotive and defence customer base gives it a natural opening for green hydrogen component work. Many of these customers will need supply chain partners who already understand forged and machined metals.
  • BF should expect Thyssenkrupp to extend its defence and aerospace reach into green hydrogen. Its technology pull into that sector could make it a direct rival for BF's defence platform suppliers.
